The desktop tool

When the charts are not enough. GST Pragyan reads the returns you download from the portal and reconciles them against each other.

  • Merges the returns. GSTR-1, 3B, 2A, 2B, 9, 9C, IFF and the e-Way Bill report, for as many periods as you have, into one workbook. Columns are matched by their heading rather than their position, so a change in the portal's export cannot silently read the wrong column.
  • Runs the scrutiny checks. Each on its own worksheet, with every column headed by the source it reads. Each tax head is tested separately, so an excess in one head is never cancelled by a shortfall in another.
  • Matches the purchases. GSTR-2B against your purchase register, invoice by invoice, with Rule 37 ageing on invoices unpaid beyond a hundred and eighty days.
  • Drafts an ASMT-10 reference. A Word draft with the statutory paragraph, the amount head by head and the month-wise working — separated into what is quantified, what needs verification first, and what is only information.

It runs on your own computer. No account, no sign-in, no upload. The returns you load and the workbooks it writes stay on the machine you run it on. The privacy policy is short.

What it will not do

A tool that overstates what it knows is worse than no tool at all.

  • It does not decide anything. Every figure is a starting point for your own examination. Nothing here is advice, and no output should be issued without being checked against the record.
  • It cannot see what is not in the returns. Whether a supply is classified correctly, whether credit was genuinely used for business, whether an e-Way Bill was a taxable supply at all — none of that is in the data, and the tool says so rather than guessing.
  • The charts are only as good as their last review. Due dates get extended at a few days' notice. The review date is at the top of the charts page. Verify anything you are about to rely on.
